# Product proposals — 2026-08-25

## User and market lens

**Primary user/job:** Swiss construction owners, contractors, project administrators, and advisers who must retrieve and rely on finalized handover/defect protocols during close-out, disputes, insurer reviews, and audits.

**Workflow moment:** A protocol has been signed and finalized. The user later needs confidence that its persisted content and signature fields cannot be edited and that the protocol cannot be individually removed through another authenticated client.

**Best-product outcome:** A best-in-market Swiss construction-compliance product should make the lifecycle boundary explicit and enforce it at the database trust boundary: finalized content/signatures are stable and authenticated users cannot individually delete a protocol, while Case unlinking, account deletion, and retention limitations remain truthful.

## S — Search and filter the finalized Protocol Register

- **Target user / JTBD:** Project administrators retrieving one finalized protocol among a growing portfolio.
- **Problem:** The register paginates past 1,000 records but offers no project, party, Case-ID, or protocol-ID search, forcing manual scanning.
- **Scope:** Add local search over already owner-scoped loaded register metadata, a clear action, result count, and multilingual empty-result copy. Do not change queries, persistence, PDFs, or Case routing.
- **Expected impact:** Faster retrieval with low implementation risk.
- **User-visible outcome:** A user can locate a known finalized record by project, party, Case ID, or protocol ID.
- **Competitive / best-product rationale:** Reliable records must also be retrievable, but search does not strengthen the legal integrity of the underlying record.
- **Risk:** 2/10.
- **Effort/size:** Small.

## M — Enforce finalized-protocol integrity at the database boundary

- **Target user / JTBD:** Owners, contractors, project teams, and advisers relying on a finalized protocol during close-out, disputes, insurer reviews, or dossier preparation.
- **Problem:** The UI calls protocols finalized and re-downloads PDFs from persisted rows, but the original owner-wide CRUD policy still permits authenticated owners to update or delete those rows through another client. The product promise is therefore stronger than the database lifecycle contract.
- **Scope:** Replace broad authenticated CRUD with owner-scoped SELECT and finalized-only INSERT; require a linked Case to belong to the inserting owner while preserving standalone inserts; remove authenticated UPDATE/DELETE table privileges and policies; retain server-controlled `finalized_at`; document that Case deletion clears the association and account deletion removes protocol records; add a concise multilingual integrity note to the Protocol Register. Preserve service-role/owner operations and all existing read paths.
- **Expected impact:** Materially stronger evidentiary trust and a truthful lifecycle boundary without redesigning protocol creation or PDFs.
- **User-visible outcome:** Users see that finalized content/signatures cannot be changed and protocols cannot be individually deleted by authenticated users; they also understand the Case-unlink and account-deletion boundaries.
- **Competitive / best-product rationale:** Database-enforced protection of finalized content/signatures and individual protocol records is a meaningful compliance differentiator; UI read-only treatment alone is insufficient.
- **Risk:** 4/10.
- **Effort/size:** Medium.
- **Phase-1 slice:** One additive migration plus truthful register copy and focused migration/UI/localization regression tests. Exclude cryptographic verification, external retention, multi-party review, delivery evidence, and PDF schema changes.

## L — Governed multi-party protocol review and countersignature lifecycle

- **Target user / JTBD:** Multi-company project teams proving who reviewed, accepted, or rejected a finalized protocol and under what authority.
- **Problem:** The current owner-scoped model cannot express reviewer authority, countersignature decisions, revocation, or cross-party audit history.
- **Scope:** Tenant membership, scoped reviewer grants, immutable protocol revisions and review decisions, countersignatures, revocation, retention, and cross-party audit trail.
- **Expected impact:** Step-change from single-owner recordkeeping to governed collaboration and defensible acceptance history.
- **User-visible outcome:** Authorized parties can review one exact immutable revision and leave a durable approve/reject decision.
- **Competitive / best-product rationale:** Governed multi-party handover is a strategic market differentiator, but it requires a deliberate authorization and privacy model.
- **Risk:** 9/10.
- **Effort/size:** Large / strategic.
- **Phase-1 slice:** Design and implement one explicitly granted reviewer for one immutable protocol revision with one append-only approve/reject decision; exclude generalized roles, invitations, notifications, delivery providers, and qualified-signature claims.
